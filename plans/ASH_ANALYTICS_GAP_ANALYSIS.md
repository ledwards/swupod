# Why the ASH human guide beats PTP's automated rankings

Comparing the manually curated ASH draft guide (Google Doc + two-page combination
rankings) against what PTP actually computes today. The question is not "can we
format it like that" — it's "why is the human analysis right and ours isn't."

## 1. The human guide is not a card power ranking

It is a **supply-vs-demand model over curve slots**, carrying seven independent
axes. Quoting the doc's own stated method:

| # | Axis | Evidence in the doc |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cost-stratified tiers** | "Cards are ranked against other cards of the same cost. S-Tier 2 drops are not necessarily as strong as S-Tier 5 drops." |
| 2 | **Demand per slot** | Ideal Number: 7–8 one/two-drops, 6 three-drops, 4–6 four-drops, 4–5 five-drops, 3 six-drops, 0–1 seven-drops |
| 3 | **Supply per slot per aspect** | The 8-way aspect ranking under *each* cost bucket (the input to the 12-combination PDF) |
| 4 | **Availability / rarity** | "The rankings after each tiering incorporate rarity as a consideration" |
| 5 | **Bomb-ness, separate from tier** | "All bombs are S-Tier, but not all S-Tier cards are bombs" |
| 6 | **Splashability** | A standalone list of cards worth the +2 aspect penalty |
| 7 | **Conditionality on leader** | Pereida Bandit S in Shin; Mortar Trooper A in Cad; Elzar Mann S with Force leaders / C otherwise; Pegasus Tri-Wing S in Greef |

Leader tiers additionally use **explicit gaps** ("Gap", "Larger Gap") rather than
fixed cutoffs, and the strategic advice falls out of the flatness: because no
leader dominates, "try to draft at least two leaders from the 9."

## 2. What PTP computes

Two separate systems, both collapsing all seven axes into **one global scalar**.

**A. Bot pick ratings** — `src/bots/data/cardRankings.ts`
Bradley-Terry percentile from real human pick contests, generated by
`scripts/analyze-pick-preferences.ts`. Controls for aspect ("within-aspect
contests"), **does not control for cost**.

**B. User-facing Card Data grades** — `src/services/cardDataMetrics.ts`,
surfaced by `src/components/CardDataTierList.tsx`
GIH/OH/GD/GNS win rates graded A+…F by z-score against the set-wide distribution
(`gradeFromZScore`, 0.33 SD per band — the 17Lands method). Note these numbers
are **not computed locally**: they are fetched from Wayfinder
(`plugin.wayfinder.news/api/cards/stats`, ASH → era `ASH-pre-release`,
`app/api/stats/card-data/route.ts:146-163`). The local DB path produces GP WR
only and hardcodes GIH/OH/GD/GNS to `null` — no per-game card-level data
(opening hands, draws) is stored in PTP at all.

Neither has a cost bucket, a demand curve, an availability weight, a bomb axis,
a splash axis, or a leader conditional. There is also **no aspect-pair rollup
anywhere** — archetypes are leader+base only
(`app/api/stats/archetype-selection/route.ts`), so the 12 combinations in the
two-page PDF cannot currently be produced by PTP at all, at any cost bucket.

## 3. Four confirmed defects, with numbers

### 3.1 Cost confound (severe, affects both systems)

Mean bot rating by cost, ASH, non-leader cards:

| Cost | n | Mean rating | In bottom 50 of set |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 27 | **16.3** | 17 |
| 2 | 64 | **42.2** | 20 |
| 3 | 45 | 56.8 | 6 |
| 4 | 32 | 63.3 | 2 |
| 5 | 24 | **67.4** | 0 |
| 6 | 20 | 60.9 | 1 |
| 7 | 20 | 52.3 | 4 |

Rating rises monotonically 1→5 drop. **37 of the 50 worst-rated cards in the set
are 1- and 2-drops** — the exact bucket the human guide says should be the
*largest* in your deck (7–8 cards). Read as a tier list, PTP's output tells you
to skip your entire early curve.

The Card Data grade list has the same shape by construction: `computeCardGrades`
is called once, globally (`app/api/stats/card-data/route.ts:518`), so expensive
cards with higher raw GIH WR occupy the A bands.

### 3.2 The underlying signal is actually good

Spearman correlation, PTP bot rating vs. human tier, computed **within** each
cost bucket:

| Cost | Matched cards | ρ |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 37 | 0.750 |
| 3 | 33 | 0.669 |
| 4 | 26 | 0.636 |
| 5 | 22 | 0.771 |
| 6 | 18 | 0.772 |
| 7 | 10 | 0.734 |

This is the most important result in this document. ρ ≈ 0.7 within bucket means
**PTP's pick data already knows what the human knows.** The disagreement is
almost entirely an artifact of ranking a 2-drop against a 5-drop. This is a
normalization bug, not a data problem, and it is cheap to fix.

### 3.3 The residual disagreement is one-directional

After controlling for cost, PTP's misses are overwhelmingly cards it
**under**rates, not overrates — Barriss Offee (human S, PTP 39), Corona Four
(S, 44), Rebel Infiltrators (A+, 39), Remnant Trooper Corps (A+, 43), Wookiee
Chieftain (A, 35), Baylan Skoll (A, 29), Stronger Together (A, 32), Enoch
(A, 34), Buy Time (A, 32), Nebulon-C Frigate (A, 38), Trexler Armored Marauder
(A, 31). Only one card goes the other way (Eye of Sion, human C, PTP 45).

These are role players and value cards, not bombs. That is the signature of
measuring **pick appeal instead of deck value**: humans take flashy cards early,
so a Bradley-Terry model over pick contests systematically underrates the
unexciting cards that actually fill a curve. This is the classic ATA-vs-GIHWR
divergence, and it is the specific thing win-rate data is supposed to correct —
which brings us to the next defect.

### 3.4 Grades are ungated noise at the sample floor

`src/services/cardDataMetrics.ts:63-65`:

```ts
export const CARD_GRADE_PRIOR_WEIGHT = 0
export const CARD_GRADE_MIN_DENOMINATOR = 50
export const CARD_GRADE_MIN_CARDS = 25
```

`priorWeight = 0` disables the shrinkage that `computeCardGrades` was written to
apply — the shrinkage line `(wins + sliceMean * priorWeight) / (denominator +
priorWeight)` reduces to the raw rate. So a card is graded on its unsmoothed win
rate as soon as it clears 50 observations.

At n = 50, the standard error on a win rate is √(0.25/50) ≈ **7.1 pp**. The
set-wide SD of card GIH WR in limited is typically 3–4 pp, and one grade band is
0.33 SD ≈ **1.3 pp**. Noise at the threshold is therefore roughly **five grade
bands wide**. Cards near the minimum sample are being assigned grades that are
essentially random, and for a set as young as ASH that is most of them.

Worse, the 50-game floor is softer than it looks on the local GP path: `gpCount`
is copy-and-match-weighted (`gpCount += copies * matches`), so a 3-of in a
5-match deck contributes 15 to the denominator while adding far less than 15
independent observations. The effective n behind a "50" is materially smaller.

**Measured against the live ASH feed (2026-07-19, draft/online):**

| | |
|---|---|
| Cards returned | 131 |
| Max `gihCount` across all cards | **43** |
| Median `gihCount` | **15** |
| Cards clearing PTP's 50-game floor | **0** |
| Cards nonetheless showing a grade | **129** (all `wayfinder-provisional`) |

Every ASH grade currently on the page is computed from a median of 15 games in
hand. At n = 15 the standard error on a win rate is ±12.9pp against grade bands
1.3pp wide — roughly **ten bands of noise**. The A+-through-D spread a user sees
on the ASH card list today is, to a first approximation, sampling noise. No
choice of bucketing fixes this; it is upstream of the cost confound and larger
than it.

### 3.5 Leaders bypass the statistics entirely

The human guide opens with leader tiers — it is the single most important section.
PTP's leader grades do not use the z-score machinery at all. They use fixed
absolute win-rate bands (`leaderGradeFromWinRate`,
`app/api/stats/card-data/route.ts:116-131`) gated only on `count > 0`. **There is
no minimum sample.** A leader with one recorded game won grades `A+`, and its
`gradeStatus` is reported as `graded`, not provisional.

Separately, the bot-side leader ordering ranks by `timesPicked` descending
(`DataDrivenBehavior.selectLeader`) — raw popularity, not strength — and ASH has
no entry in `LEADER_RANKINGS` at all, so it depends entirely on that live
popularity signal.

## 4. Fixes, ordered by leverage

**1. Stratify grading by cost bucket.** `computeCardGrades` already accepts a
`sliceMean` option. Call it once per cost bucket (1–2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7+) instead
of once globally, and label the output "S-tier 3-drop", not "A+". This alone
converts a ρ≈0.7 signal from unusable to broadly correct, and it is a small
change at `app/api/stats/card-data/route.ts:518`. Do the same when generating
`cardRankings.ts`.

**2. Turn shrinkage on.** Set `CARD_GRADE_PRIOR_WEIGHT` to a real value (start
near the median denominator) so low-sample cards are pulled toward the bucket
mean rather than flung to the tails. Consider raising `MIN_DENOMINATOR` and
showing a confidence band rather than a letter for anything thin.

**3. Blend the pick prior with the win-rate posterior.** Pick data (§3.2)
stabilizes in dozens of drafts; GIH WR needs thousands of games. A new set has
the former and not the latter. Grade on a weighted blend whose weight shifts
toward win rate as n grows — that is also the mechanism that corrects the
one-directional bias in §3.3, since win rate is exactly what rewards the role
players pick order underrates.

**4. Add a demand curve.** `avgCostCurve` exists in `src/bots/data/draftStats.ts`
but is descriptive — the average curve of decks people *built*. The human guide's
"Ideal Number" is prescriptive. Recompute it from **winning** decks
(`built_decks` joined to match results) to produce a real target, then express
each bucket's rating relative to that demand.

**5. Derive the per-cost aspect rankings.** With 1 and 4 in place this is
mechanical: for each (aspect, cost) sum the quality of available cards, weight by
pack availability, rank. PTP has a genuine advantage here that no human has — it
generates the packs, so it knows the exact collation odds and can compute
availability weighting (the guide's axis 4) precisely instead of by feel.

**6. Split bomb-ness from tier.** A bomb is high variance and wins games from
behind; a good card is high mean. Candidate metric: win rate when drawn late, or
GIH WR conditional on being behind on board. Distinct column, not a higher grade.

**7. Splash axis.** Compare a card's win rate on-aspect vs. off-aspect. PTP can
measure this directly from deck aspects; the human guessed it.

**8. Leader conditionals.** `PerLeaderCardStats` already exists in
`draftStats.ts`. Surface the largest card×leader deltas rather than hiding them
in a global average — that is what produces "Elzar Mann is S-Tier with Force
leaders, C-Tier otherwise."

**9. Fix leader grading, then make it gap-aware.** First put leaders on the same
sample gating as cards — the current `count > 0` floor (§3.5) is the most
embarrassing single defect in the stack, and it sits on the guide's most-read
section. Then cluster leaders by rating and cut tiers at the largest gaps instead
of fixed thresholds, which is what reproduces the guide's "Gap" / "Larger Gap"
rows and the "draft at least two of the 9" conclusion.

**10. Record what was passed.** `draft_picks` stores the chosen card but not the
rest of the pack, so PTP has no ALSA / last-seen-at — the single best-converging
signal in limited analytics. `scripts/analyze-pick-preferences.ts` already
reconstructs pack contents offline via `reconstructDraftLog`, which proves the
data is recoverable; persisting seen-cards at pick time would make ALSA a
first-class metric. ALSA plus cost stratification is most of what the human guide
encodes about card availability.

## 5. The strategic point

Items 1–3 are corrections to existing code and are where almost all the immediate
accuracy is. Items 4–9 are new axes and are where the *insight* is: they are the
difference between a sorted list of cards and a document that tells you how to
draft.

Worth stating plainly: a win-rate-first analytics stack will never reproduce this
guide at set launch, because the guide was written from card text and a few dozen
games before meaningful win-rate data existed. The structural axes (cost buckets,
demand curve, availability weighting, aspect supply) need **zero** game data.
They should be the backbone at set release, with win-rate data arriving later as
a correction — not the other way around.
